  what: >-
    Two families do almost everything, pushed to opposite ends of their
    weight ranges. Signifier, a sharp, modern take on an old-style serif,
    sets the big italic "Everything" in the selector (Bold, 44px) and the
    card descriptions, down to a Light weight for event titles. GT America,
    a grotesque sans, sets card titles in its heaviest weight, Black, and
    small labels and dates in Light and Regular. A third font, The Future
    (a geometric sans after Futura), appears only as tiny spaced-out
    capitals for the card type labels running up the side of each card
    ("STORY", "TOOL", "WORKSHOP").
  why: >-
    Firebelly says the d.school "sought typographic energy and visual
    variety": "Rather than repeating the logo's use of The Future, we
    leveraged GT America and Signifier as our primary fonts across text and
    pages, pushing their weights to extremes to create a bold, expressive
    visual rhythm that establishes clear hierarchy". The pairing is meant
    to balance "academic credibility with creative spirit". Klim describes
    Signifier as "a Brutalist response to 17th-century typefaces", and The
    Future as "a homage to Futura". (Our read: the Black sans titles shout
    and the Light serif text talks quietly, so every card has a clear
    headline even at small sizes.)

  what: >-
    Overall, the imagery is a messy worktable: cards tossed into a pile,
    rough painted edges and real photos of people mid-workshop, all on
    plain paper.

    On arrival the cards land as a card pile, tilted at slight angles,
    under a red banner with a rough edge like a dry-brush stroke; the dark
    footer has the same ragged top. Below, the cards settle into a grid of
    color-blocked tiles. Photos are candid lifestyle photography of
    students and teams at work, and cards without a photo show blurred abstract
    shapes in a soft gradient under a fine halftone dot screen. Hovering a card turns
    its photo red and pink, a duotone, and the footer ends with a big
    hand-painted red exclamation mark.
  why: >-
    Firebelly explains the overall idea: "The d.school's hands-on
    collaborative approach and emphasis on analog, messy ideation formed
    our foundation." The team "used the magic circle as inspiration for
    distinctive image treatments with halftone overlays and textural
    adjustments", and "incorporated sketches and gestural marks connecting
    copy and imagery".

    In detail: the studio wanted the site to "inspire people to 'step into
    the magic circle' of design", and the homepage concept they chose was
    "The Center", "a digital crossroads connecting diverse ideas and
    people". (Our read: the tossed card pile is that crossroads, a table
    covered in everyone's work, and the real workshop photos keep it about
    people rather than polished marketing.)

  what: >-
    On load, an intro animation fades in the selector, then drops the
    cards onto the page one after another in a staggered pile, and sweeps
    the red banner in underneath. Moving the mouse draws a cursor trail: a
    thin red line with a dot that follows the pointer and fades, while the
    card under it switches to its red duotone hover state. Picking a topic
    in the "I'm curious about…" selector runs a filter transition: the pile
    and banner clear away and the matching cards fade into a grid one by
    one. Further down, cards wait off-screen and fade in with a staggered
    scroll-reveal as each row comes into view.
  why: >-
    Firebelly says "cursor trails mimicking analog mark-making" are a nod
    to the d.school's hands-on ideation, and describes the homepage as "an
    interactive journey shaped by visitor interests", where "cards
    rearrange and repopulate to showcase pages, stories, people, and
    events". (Our read: dealing the cards out one by one feels like someone
    laying out work on a table for you, which suits a school that teaches
    by doing.)
